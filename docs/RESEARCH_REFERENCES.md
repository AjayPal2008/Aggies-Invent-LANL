## Learning science and learning styles

> Note on learning styles: research does not support the “meshing” idea that teaching to a person's preferred style
> (for example, visual or auditory) improves learning (Pashler et al., 2008; Rohrer & Pashler, 2012; Kirschner, 2017;
> Husmann & O'Loughlin, 2019). The platform therefore treats a stated style as a starting preference only, and adapts
> to measured results instead: knowledge tracing (Corbett & Anderson, 1994), a multi-armed bandit that keeps the
> delivery formats that actually work for each person (Clement et al., 2015), retrieval practice and spacing
> (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006; Cepeda et al., 2006; Dunlosky et al., 2013), multimedia design principles (Mayer, 2020),
> and situational interviews modeled on the multiple mini-interview and CASPer (Eva et al., 2004; Dore et al., 2009).
